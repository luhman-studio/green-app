How that compares

Tonnes CO₂e per person per year, consumption-based, stacked by area. Austria and world are published averages, so only your own bar carries a range. The dashed line is the 1.5 °C-aligned lifestyle footprint for 2030.

Where the range comes from, and why it is not centred

It is not a measurement error — nothing here was measured. Your answers are recalculated 4,000 times over, and on each run every emission factor and every quantity you were unsure about is redrawn somewhere inside its published uncertainty. The bracket covers the middle 80% of those runs: one in ten lands below it, one in ten above.

The top of the bar is the central estimate, and the bracket usually reaches further above it than below. That is not a drawing error: these uncertainties multiply rather than add, so they stretch the top more than the bottom.

It is an uncertainty interval rather than a box plot on purpose. A box plot summarises a distribution of observations — quartiles, whiskers, outliers, many things measured. There is one of you and one footprint; the spread is how unsure the calculator is about a single value, not variation between people.

Answering “exact” instead of “rough guess” on the questions marked big effect is what makes it narrower.

How to read this diagram

Read it left to right: what causes the emissions (the fuel, the electricity, the farm, the factory) → what you do with it → which area it belongs to → your whole footprint. Every band’s height is exactly its tonnes and the columns have no gaps, so the picture is the arithmetic — which means a small thing looks small.
